Every net-newly-proved obligation came directly out of `Unproved`, not out
of `Unsupported` (which is unchanged to the obligation) — the signature of a
fix that makes previously-attempted-but-failed SMT translations succeed,
not one that widens the set of expressions AdaLang is willing to attempt.
This, plus the per-kind breakdown below, points at `f8a0375`, not `a6239c1`.

### Why `a6239c1` (loop-variant) moved nothing here

SPARKNaCl has exactly 10 `loop-variant` obligations
(`sparknacl-aes.adb:641/683/714/743`, `sparknacl-hashing-rfsb509.adb:129/211`,
`sparknacl-hashing-sha256.adb:168`, `sparknacl-hashing-sha2_common.adb:193`,
`sparknacl-mac.adb:96/102`). All 10 are `Unproved` in this run — zero moved
to `Proved_Safe`. This is not a coincidence-of-null-result; it is a direct
consequence of how `a6239c1` gates the new proof. In
`Adalang_Analyzer.Flow_Interp`, `Mark_Variant_Progress` only calls
`VC.Decide_Variant_Progress` when `Invariants_Usable_For_Variant (Header)`
holds — i.e. only when the loop's own leading `Loop_Invariant` has already
been discharged (both initialization and preservation `VC_Discharged`).
SPARKNaCl's own `loop-invariant-initialization` and
`loop-invariant-preservation` obligations are 0/81 `Proved_Safe` each in
this run (26 `Unproved` + 55 `Unsupported` apiece) — no loop header in this
corpus ever satisfies that precondition, so every leading loop-variant
check falls straight to `VC_Not_Discharged` without `Decide_Variant_Progress`
ever running. The bucket didn't move because its prerequisite bucket
(loop-invariant preservation) never fires on this corpus, not because the
new solver-backed proof failed to close the goals it was given.

### Why `f8a0375` (scalar-sort resolution) plausibly explains the swing

`f8a0375` replaces two weak heuristics with real semantic type resolution:
`Formal_Sort` previously guessed Boolean-vs-Integer from a formal's type
mark **text** (`"boolean"` spelling only), and enum-vs-integer objects were
distinguished only by whether `Flow_Domain` already had range facts for
them — both of which could silently mis-sort a term, tripping
`Sort_Mismatch` and derailing translation. The fix instead resolves through
`P_Canonical_Fully_Qualified_Name = "standard.boolean"`,
`P_Is_Int_Type`, and `P_Is_Enum_Type`. Notably, the diff also deletes a
dead branch: the old code's own comment on `Formal_Sort` says "`Formal_Sort`
never returns `Enum_Sort`" — meaning every enum-typed **actual parameter**
was previously routed to `Mark_Unsupported (Sort_Mismatch)` unconditionally,
regardless of whether the callee could otherwise have handled it.

SPARKNaCl itself declares **no user-defined enumeration types** (`grep -rn
"type.*is ("` over `src/*.ads` is empty), so the "enum-typed assignment"
soundness gap this commit closes is realized here through `Standard.Boolean`
— which is semantically an enumeration type in Ada, and which SPARKNaCl
uses heavily: 66 references across the corpus, including `out Boolean`/`in
Boolean` formal parameters (`Status`, `Swap`, `Xor_M`) and `Boolean'Val`/
`'Pos` conversions in constant-time swap logic (`sparknacl-scalar.adb`,
`sparknacl-utils.adb`). Before the fix, a formal like `Swap : in Boolean`
resolved correctly only because its type mark's spelling happened to be
`"boolean"`; a Boolean value reached through an intermediate expression,
record field, or non-literally-spelled subtype was exactly the class of
case the old heuristic could mis-sort. Per-kind, this run's largest
`Proved_Safe` contributors are `range-check` (553/2,045) and `index-check`
(498/1,016) — exactly the checks that benefit when a Boolean-guarded
branch's assumptions propagate correctly into SPARKNaCl's array-bounded
scalar arithmetic, rather than being dropped at a `Sort_Mismatch`. This
reading is consistent with, not confirmed by, the aggregate deltas — there
is no archived per-obligation JSON from the 2026-08-11 run to diff against
line-for-line (`benchmark-results/` is gitignored, same caveat as every
prior entry in this series).

## Bottom line

Real, positive movement, and the numbers that matter most stayed at zero:
`Proved_Safe` gained 400 obligations (37 → 53 of them in the strict
matched-pair "both safe" sense), `Unsupported` held exactly flat at 1,512,
and both risk buckets — possible unsoundness and false positive — are still
bit-identical zero across every run of this corpus to date. The evidence
points at `f8a0375`'s scalar-sort fix as the driver, operating here mainly
through SPARKNaCl's extensive `Standard.Boolean` usage rather than
user-defined enums (SPARKNaCl declares none). `a6239c1`'s loop-variant fix,
by contrast, produced a confirmed null result on this corpus: all 10
`loop-variant` obligations remain `Unproved`, traced to the fix's own
gating on a discharged leading `Loop_Invariant` — a precondition SPARKNaCl's
loop-invariant obligations (0/81 `Proved_Safe`) never satisfy here. That is
a corpus-shape limitation, not a defect in the new proof itself.

## Follow-up, same day: `Is_Leading_Invariant` order fix (FP-050) re-checked

A same-session follow-up (`quality/known_analysis_issues.tsv` FP-050,
found while re-running `project_bias`) fixed a real bug in exactly the
gating this section describes: `Is_Leading_Invariant` only recognized a
`Loop_Invariant` as leading when every preceding loop-body statement was
itself a `loop-invariant` pragma — a preceding `loop-variant` pragma broke
that check, regardless of the invariant's own content. SPARKNaCl's loops
consistently write `Loop_Variant` before `Loop_Invariant` (e.g.
`sparknacl-aes.adb:641`), the exact shape the bug disqualified.

Re-checked after the fix: `Proved_Safe` is unchanged (3,869), and the
`loop-invariant-preservation` obligations at these sites now report a
genuine, specific failure ("the loop body does not establish invariant
preservation") in place of the earlier non-leading skip — confirming the
fix activates correctly here too — but still don't discharge, blocked by
the invariants' own content (e.g. `(Round_Keys (Round_Keys'First ..
Key_Index)'Initialized)`, an array-attribute predicate outside the
straight-line scalar VC subset this engine supports, not by pragma
order). The "0/81 `Proved_Safe`" figure above and its attribution to
SPARKNaCl's invariant complexity stands; the ordering bug this section
originally flagged as a contributing factor is now ruled out specifically,
not merely suspected.
