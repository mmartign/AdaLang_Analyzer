## What changed since 2026-09-30

The same 9,484 obligations as before; 714 moved to `Proved_Safe` and 43
moved from `Proved_Safe` to `Unproved`.

- Newly proved: 220 overflow checks, 192 initialization checks, 178 range
  checks, 123 index checks and 1 assertion. They come from what 1.6.1 added:
  membership tests and `for` loops over an object's own range narrow
  intervals, array bounds that no declaration fixes are terms the provers
  reason about, and a call to a function in SPARK or in a `Pure` unit, or to
  a predefined attribute, no longer discards what is known, initialization
  included.
- No longer proved: 27 range checks, 14 preconditions, 1 index check and 1
  postcondition. They rested on what 1.6.1 no longer assumes: the bounds of
  an array that no declaration fixes (`FP-088`), and facts about objects
  that a call, a loop or an alias can change (`FP-090`-`FP-094`). The
  2026-09-30 comparison found none of them wrong (zero possible unsoundness
  then as now); they are withdrawn because the reasoning behind them was not
  sound in general.

18 matched pairs moved from AdaLang-conservative to both-safe. The four
"both flag" pairs are still GNATprove's own `medium` messages in
`sparknacl-sign.adb`, where AdaLang is `Unsupported`.

One `Constant_Condition` finding of the previous `--verify` run is gone: it
was reported from a loop's state before the fixed point converged
(`FP-095`).

The analyzer lane took 30 s against 87 s last run: most solver processes are
no longer started (see `CHANGELOG.md`, 1.6.2).

## Analyzer refresh 2026-10-08 (release 1.8.4)

The analyzer side was re-run on 2026-10-08 with release 1.8.4. It raises
three obligations 1.8.3 did not have: the length check of an array given to
a target, a kind of its own; the range check on `X'Length` where it is
converted to an integer type; and the precondition of an operator that a
declaration defines. A length is known to be no more than the number of
values of its index subtype. It also fixes `FP-116`, a false-safe that shows
in no corpus, whose declared operators compute what their symbol says: such
an operator was read as the predefined operation, and its calls carried the
overflow and division checks of that operation. An array that is assigned or
declared no longer carries a range check on its value, which meant nothing.
GNATprove was not re-run: its 2026-10-02 output was compared again as saved,
